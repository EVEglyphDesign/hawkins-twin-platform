<h2>3. The executive dashboard — the ticket to scale</h2>
<p>Tim already runs his business against a shared numeric language: the composite his 20 Group of Peterbilt dealers compares itself on, alongside the ratios PACCAR issues. He reads it, he trusts it, and he already talks to his peers in it. That makes it the only sensible front end for the twin.</p>
<p class="q">"Tim knows what those numbers are and he's used to looking at them. He's already talking to his friends about them. So on the front end of whatever we propose, we need to take them from where they are to where they're going — and start with something he understands."</p>

<h3>3.1 The design rule</h3>
<p>The twin does not publish a new set of metrics. <b>It lands its output in the composite's own cells, under the composite's own names.</b> Every workstream in the register above must terminate in a line item Tim can point at in a room full of peers without translating anything. If a piece of work cannot be expressed as movement in a composite line, it is not yet a business outcome — it is still a technical output.</p>

<h3>3.2 Structure</h3>
<table>
<tr><th style="width:20%">Layer</th><th style="width:38%">What it shows</th><th>Source</th></tr>
<tr><td><b>Composite mirror</b></td><td>Tim's line items exactly as the 20 Group publishes them, current period against group average, group top quartile, and Hawkins' own trailing twelve months.</td><td>ADA composite export (HT-01) + PACCAR ratio pack</td></tr>
<tr><td><b>Twin overlay</b></td><td>The same lines, restated from the twin's live data — so the composite stops being a monthly rear-view and becomes a running position.</td><td>CDK / Lightspeed structure mirror; inventory twin</td></tr>
<tr><td><b>Gap and cause</b></td><td>For every line below group average: the drivers underneath it, ranked by the dollars they explain. This is the part the composite itself cannot do.</td><td>Twin reasoning layer</td></tr>
<tr><td><b>Action queue</b></td><td>The specific, owned, dated moves that would close each gap — transfer this stock, chase this warranty claim, answer this call queue — with the projected line-item effect of each.</td><td>Twin recommendations, human-approved</td></tr>
<tr><td><b>Provenance strip</b></td><td>For every number: where it came from, when it refreshed, and which contract permits reading it. This is what makes it defensible in front of peers and in front of vendors.</td><td>Contract register (HT-06) + ledger</td></tr>
</table>

<h3>3.4 Why it is the ticket to scale</h3>
<p>The 20 Group is a room of dealer principals comparing themselves on identical lines. A tool that speaks that language needs no explanation there — Tim's peers can see their own position in it on first sight. The path is therefore: Tim's own numbers first, understood by Tim, defended by Tim; then the same instrument offered to the peers, on their own sovereign tenants, under their own names. That is where the recurring economics live, and it is the position Salesforce currently occupies in dealer groups of that size.</p>

<h2>4. Two directions at once</h2>
<table>
<tr><th style="width:16%">Direction</th><th style="width:42%">What it does</th><th>Proof it produces</th></tr>
<tr><td><b>Top-down</b></td><td>Start at the composite. Mirror the numbers Tim already reports to his peers, then push causation and an action queue underneath them.</td><td>Tim can walk into the 20 Group with a live position rather than a monthly report. That conversation is the distribution channel.</td></tr>
<tr><td><b>Bottom-up</b></td><td>Take the operational irritations that exist today — missed calls, stock sitting in the wrong centre, a bolt-on nobody uses — and solve them in the mesh, cheaply, one at a time.</td><td>Each one is evidence the mesh design works on real Hawkins conditions, not on a slide. Each one also funds itself by removing cost.</td></tr>
</table>
<p>They meet in the middle: every bottom-up fix is scored by which composite line it moved, and every top-down gap is worked by handing it to the mesh as a bottom-up problem. Neither direction is allowed to run without the other — top-down alone is a dashboard, bottom-up alone is a pile of scripts.</p>

<h2>5. The Tobias lane — sovereign repository with custom stacks</h2>
<p>The division of labour stated on the call: Dany moves the old world into the new one and handles the humans; Tobias structures the new world so it runs efficiently. Tobias's work is deliberately routed to the funding lane rather than the invoice, so it does not consume Tim's $10K.</p>
<ul>
<li><b>The shape.</b> A sovereign repository as the system of record, on Hawkins' Azure subscription, with custom stacks sitting on top of it. Tim's own read of it on the call: <i>"I could actually take this and build my own business system… within a week and I say goodbye to CDK."</i></li>
<li><b>The funding logic.</b> Sovereignty is a precondition, not a preference. Work that rests on another vendor's IP does not qualify — that is precisely what blocked an earlier funding attempt built on an SAP product. Owning the model outright is what makes SR&amp;ED, OEM R&amp;D programmes and export funding reachable.</li>
<li><b>The commercial position.</b> The subscription is inverted: priced against what the incumbent charges, reduced year over year, with paid work only for genuinely new custom stacks — and every custom stack built for one dealer becomes available to the rest. Hawkins owns the asset and resells it; the developer is back end, not owner.</li>
<li><b>The channel.</b> Making the OEM-side SAP estate work better positions SAP as a channel into other Peterbilt dealerships rather than an obstacle. Salesforce is the displacement target, not SAP.</li>
</ul>

<h2>6. What gets caught in the mesh, and what gets written down</h2>
<p>Anything not routed to Tobias is handled directly in the mesh — extracted, structured into the repository, and made queryable, fast and unbilled. The discipline that makes that safe is the record:</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Contracts first.</b> No system is touched before its agreement is in the repository and read. Ambiguity becomes a written question to the vendor, and the vendor's answer goes back into the model as evidence.</li>
<li><b>Structure, not operations.</b> Database structure only, taken from backup and recovery systems. No live-system risk, no code copied, no copyright exposure.</li>
<li><b>The ledger.</b> Every action, every access, every vendor answer, every failure — including EVEglyph's own — is logged with a timestamp and a source. Seven defects from a single day in July are already on file. The ledger exists so that if a platform, a vendor or a partner behaves badly, the record is contemporaneous, attributable and usable. It is an accountability instrument first and a legal instrument second.</li>
<li><b>Exit.</b> Hawkins can copy the repository and leave at any moment, with everything. That is the guarantee Tim asked for, and it is structural rather than contractual.</li>
</ul>
