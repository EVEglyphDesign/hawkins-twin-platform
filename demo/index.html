The governed path

This is the part that matters more than the dashboard. The twin never writes to the system of record. It drafts. A named human approves. The posting flows out through a standard interface and lands in an audit trail that reconciles.

Three tiers, twinned from the MHC pattern

Tier 1
Intelligent execution

The agent and operations console. Reads inventory events, classifies, forecasts demand, flags stockout and overstock, recommends inter-centre transfers. This is the surface you are looking at.

Tier 2
System of record

The existing DMS or parts system plus governed middleware. Parts master, VIN records, stock levels, orders. The AI never writes directly — every posting flows through a validated, human-approved path.

Tier 3
Analytics

Dashboards feeding a datalake. Inventory turns, fill rate, aged stock, inter-centre balancing, margin by part class. The KPI strip at the top of this page is the Tier 3 view.

What this demo does and does not prove

It proves that the twin's model, reorder logic, transfer engine, approval gate, and audit trail run end to end and produce legible operating decisions — without anyone at Hawkins granting a single system credential.
It does not prove anything about Hawkins' actual fill rate, turns, or aged stock. Those numbers are unknowable until the twin reads real data from one centre. The sandbox is a demonstration of mechanism, not a measurement of the business.

How the synthetic data was made

  • A single seeded Python generator — generate_sample_data.py, seed 20260727 — committed beside this page. Re-run it and you get exactly this dataset.
  • Ten part classes with plausible unit-cost bands, base monthly demand, and criticality; nine centres with a size factor and a seasonal factor.
  • Months-of-supply on hand drawn from a mixed distribution: thin, tight, healthy, heavy. Risk state is derived, not assigned.
  • Static ROP = demand × 0.9, ignoring lead time. Twin ROP = demand × (lead time ÷ 30) × a criticality safety factor, plus a floor for critical lines.
  • Transfer recommendations are generated only where a genuine shortage at one centre coincides with genuine surplus at another for the same part number.
  • VINs are random 17-character strings. They decode to nothing.

The research questions this sandbox sets up

  • Can an open, vendor-independent twin meet the same intelligent asset-management requirements on live dealership inventory that incumbent platforms have not met, with a small team?
  • Can demand-pattern recognition across nine geographically distributed centres outperform static reorder points on fill rate and turns, under real seasonal and regional variance?
  • Can the governed AI → middleware → system-of-record path guarantee audit integrity and upgrade safety while operating with zero lock-in?

Each is a genuine "we do not know until we experiment" question — which is what makes the work qualifying R&D rather than tool adoption.

What the pilot would need, and what it would not

Needed

  • Read access to parts and vehicle inventory data for one centre — Hanwell — to start.
  • A named operations owner for approvals during the pilot.
  • The existing system's interface details: DMS or parts system, and any PACCAR parts feed.
  • Luke as research-tool lead bridging Hawkins and the EVE AI team.

Not needed

  • No write access at pilot stage. The twin reads; postings stay manual until the governed path is signed off.
  • No change to how any staff member works. The existing dealer system keeps its operational role.
  • No proprietary connector, and nothing that holds the data hostage on exit.
  • No credential, export, or document that any vendor agreement does not already permit.
